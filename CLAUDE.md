# Spartan Systems Marketing Site

Static marketing site (home page, privacy policy, terms of service and SMS terms) served by GitHub Pages at **spartansystems.us** (apex domain via `CNAME` file). No build step, no framework, no dependencies.

- `privacy/index.html` is the privacy policy (served at `/privacy/`), linked from the footer here and from the app. Keep it in step with what the product actually collects and which providers receive data.
- `terms/index.html` is the Terms of Service (`/terms/`), linked from every footer and given to Twilio as the toll-free verification's terms URL. Keep its plans, trial and cancelling text in step with the pricing section and the app's billing, and its SMS section in step with `/sms/`.
- `sms/index.html` is the SMS Alerts Terms (`/sms/`), the public opt-in proof for Twilio toll-free verification, linked from the app's consent box. Its consent text, confirmation and HELP replies must match the app's Notifications section and spartan-api's `SmsProgram`; the `sms/opt-in-*.png` screenshots show the app's opt-in steps.
- Everything else lives in `index.html`: inline CSS (custom properties like `--navy`, `--blue2`) and one inline script (clip playback, the hero search, the live counts). The layout (SPAR-240): search-first hero, full-width product shot with the counts under it, a card grid for the modules, and split heading/content sections. `logo-ink.png` and `logo-white.png` are transparent crops of `spartan_logo.png` for the light nav and the dark footer. External resources are Google Fonts (Inter), the Google Ads tag (`AW-17734229904`, in the `<head>` of every page, ad personalization off; the policy's cookies section depends on that), and PostHog (same project and token as the app, `cross_subdomain_cookie` so data.spartansystems.us continues the visitor's ID; covered by the policy's PostHog lines).
- All CTAs point to the product app: Log In to `https://data.spartansystems.us/login`; the trial buttons to `https://data.spartansystems.us/start-trial` (government-data's route: Create Account when signed out, then the plan picker after onboarding); the hero search to `/search` with `niin`, `partNumber` or `itemName` by its tab, the parameters government-data's SearchPage reads (its script cuts a 13-digit NSN to the NIIN; the button carries `data-ph-capture-attribute-search-type`); contact is `mailto:sales@spartansystems.us`. Each trial button carries `data-ph-capture-attribute-placement` for PostHog.
- The hero's "Type your CAGE" box (SPAR-314) calls spartan-api's anonymous `GET /api/Site/cage-report?cage=` (CORS allows this origin) and shows the company's open RFQ counts and three lines, or "We couldn't find DLA awards for that CAGE.". Its "See all" button opens the app's `/start-trial?cage=&company=`, which stores them for sign-up and onboarding (the app is another origin, so they travel in the address). It sends `cage_report_requested` and `cage_report_signup` to PostHog. `site-config.js` holds the API and app bases and `turnstileSiteKey`: empty, the box sends no Cloudflare token, and the API checks one only once it has the matching secret. The privacy policy's CAGE lookups line goes with it.
- The counts line under the product shot and the two DIBBS coverage cards fill in dated counts from spartan-api's anonymous `GET /api/Site/stats` (CORS allows this origin for that route only). If the call fails the static text stays.
- The `screen_*.webp` screenshots are of the `government-data` app and go stale when its UI changes. The 2026-11-05 set was taken as the QA account through a read-only proxy to the prod API, personalized to CAGE 6JSD9, a small fastener supplier. Its name is kept out, but the CAGE shows on the dashboard and the pricing vendor table, since both are public award data. `screen_leo.webp` was replaced by `clip_leo.mp4` in SPAR-237. The bid board is made up from that CAGE's real open solicitations and DIBBS awards, with closed lines in the automatic "Awarded on DIBBS" state. Leo's answers on the page are real, asked that day and shortened. `screen_pricing.webp` was retaken on 2026-11-06 (SPAR-218, the confidence chip removed) on the live app as the QA account, same NSN 5306-01-045-6594 and framing. The NSN page doesn't read the user's CAGE, so that shot needs no proxy. `screen_solicitations.webp` was retaken on 2026-11-15, after government-data#192 fixed the clipped select-all checkbox. It was shot on the live app as the QA account, with no proxy, because the list doesn't read the CAGE. The window was 1440×900 at 2x, scaled to 2000×1250 WebP q86.
- **Plan prices** are plain text in `index.html`, in three places: the counts line under the product shot ("From $…"), the pricing panel's seat table and the final CTA ("from $… per month"). They must match what checkout charges and the app's `/features` page.
- `og-image.png` (1200×630), `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml` sit at the root. The description, `og:description` and `twitter:description` strings and the image's "20M+" text change together, along with the app's `public/index.html` copy.
- Deploy = push to the default branch (Pages serves the branch directly; there is no Actions workflow).
- `clip_*.mp4` are muted loops (Leo, bid board quote file, search to NSN pricing) recorded 2026-11-13 with the same QA proxy and CAGE 6JSD9: headless Chrome screencast at 2x, cropped to one panel, encoded to H.264 by Chrome's MediaRecorder and remuxed with macOS `avconvert -p PresetPassthrough` so the file has a duration. Each is under 1 MB; `clip_*.webp` is its poster. The page's script plays a clip only while it is on screen and leaves it on its poster under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Every element with `data-expand` (the dashboard shot, the module cards, Leo's clip) gets an Expand button that opens it in the `#viewer` dialog, where clips play with controls; the button's `data-ph-capture-attribute-expand` names the item for PostHog.
